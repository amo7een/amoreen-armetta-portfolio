Case study · AI-assisted content operations

Designing and implementing a content operations system

I redesigned the documentation workflow so AI could handle repetitive research and analysis while writers retained editorial judgment.

Role Senior Technical Writer

Focus Content operations, workflow design, AI assistance

Impact Designed and operationalized AI-assisted workflows that automated research and scoping work previously requiring approximately three hours per writer per day across a three-person team.

01

The problem

Technical writers spent hours piecing together product context

Information about upcoming releases was spread across engineering tickets, Slack conversations, product briefs, design specs, meeting notes, and support feedback.

Before writers could scope or draft documentation, they had to answer:

  • What changed?
  • Which articles were affected?
  • What needed to be created or updated?
  • How did the changes affect the help center as a whole?

02

The system

AI agents surfaced the work. Writers made the decisions.

I designed an AI-assisted workflow using API calls, agent triggers, and automated actions.

Collect product info

Search product briefs, engineering work, conversations, and release information.

↓
Identify documentation impact

Identify product changes, affected content, gaps, and conflicting information.

↓
Generate a first draft

Create a draft and flag potential terminology, style, accessibility, and consistency issues.

↓
Review and publish

Writers verify product behavior, determine user needs, restructure and rewrite the content. Then writers confirm accuracy with subject-matter experts, ensure article fits with overall content strategy, and manually publish.

03

The result

A faster, more consistent documentation operation

Automated research and scoping Automated work that had previously required approximately three hours per writer per day across a three-person team.
Earlier visibility into upcoming product changes
More consistent terminology, style, and accessibility
Increased capacity for gathering metrics, content strategy, and help center design

How the baseline was measured: Based on ticket time-tracking data from the three-person writing team, which showed roughly three hours a day spent searching engineering roadmaps and backlogs, Slack conversations, ticket comments, and product briefs, and asking subject matter experts questions, to understand exactly what was being updated.

Pricing and packaging

Building a reusable content system

Some documentation problems require changing how the information is organized.

Problem

Plan, pricing, and permission details were repeated across dozens of help center articles. Each product change required multiple manual updates.

Approach

Designed a custom reusable content system so availability and limits information could be defined once and referenced across every article in the feature group, rather than duplicated in each article.

I designed a reusable content model in Zendesk and styled it with custom CSS to match ClickUp’s brand.

Result

Pricing and packaging changes required one update instead of dozens, reducing maintenance work and content drift.
