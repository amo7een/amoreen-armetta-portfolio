Case study · AI-assisted content operations

Designing and implementing a content operations system

I led the design and operationalization of an AI-assisted documentation workflow. As the workflow architect and project lead, I defined how the agents, data sources, editorial steps, and quality controls worked together. Other members of the writing team made significant contributions, including building and refining individual agents.

Role Workflow architect and project lead

Focus Content operations, workflow design, AI assistance

Impact Designed and operationalized AI-assisted workflows that automated research and scoping work previously requiring approximately three hours per writer per day across a three-person team.

01

The problem

Technical writers spent hours piecing together product context

Information about upcoming releases was spread across engineering tickets, Slack conversations, product briefs, design specs, meeting notes, and support feedback.

Before writers could scope or draft documentation, they had to answer:

  • What changed?
  • Which articles were affected?
  • What needed to be created or updated?
  • How did the changes affect the help center as a whole?

02

The system

AI agents surfaced the work. Writers made the decisions.

I designed the overall workflow architecture and led its implementation with the writing team. We used API calls, agent triggers, and automated actions to gather product context, identify documentation gaps, and generate draft starting points. Team members contributed to the design and development of individual agents, while I focused on the end-to-end system, handoffs, editorial controls, and operational rollout.

Collect product context

Agents review product briefs, engineering tickets, Slack conversations, and release information.

↓
Identify documentation impact

Agents identify product changes, affected content, gaps, and conflicting information.

↓
Generate a starting point

Agents create a draft and flag potential terminology, style, accessibility, and consistency issues.

↓
Review and publish

Writers verify product behavior, identify user needs, restructure and rewrite the draft, confirm accuracy with subject-matter experts, align the article with the broader content strategy, and publish the final content.

03

The result

A more scalable, consistent documentation operation

Automated research and documentation scoping
Earlier visibility into upcoming product changes
More consistency across terminology, style, and accessibility
Increased capacity for gathering metrics, content strategy, and help center design

How the baseline was measured: Ticket time-tracking data showed that each writer spent roughly three hours a day reviewing engineering roadmaps and backlogs, Slack conversations, ticket comments, and product briefs, and consulting subject-matter experts to understand what was changing.

Pricing and packaging

Building a reusable content system

Some documentation problems require changing how the information is organized.

Problem

Plan, pricing, and permission details were repeated across dozens of help center articles. Each product change required multiple manual updates.

Approach

I designed a reusable content model in Zendesk that centralized each feature group’s plan, pricing, and permission details in a single source article. Every related article included a callout linking to that source. I styled the callout with custom CSS to match ClickUp’s brand.

Result

When the information changed, writers updated one source article instead of every article in the feature group. This reduced duplicate maintenance and helped keep the guidance consistent.
